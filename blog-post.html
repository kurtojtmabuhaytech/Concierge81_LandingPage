How AI concierges cut front-desk wait times by half.

A practical look at what hotel guests actually ask — and how a chat-and-voice concierge takes the repetitive 80% off the desk.

What guests actually ask

We reviewed six months of front-desk logs from partner hotels. Most questions weren't complicated — they were the same twenty questions, asked hundreds of times a week: Wi-Fi passwords, breakfast hours, late check-out, airport transfers.

Each one takes a staff member less than a minute to answer. Together, they add up to hours of queue time during check-in peaks — exactly when guests are least patient.

Tip

Start with your top 20 questions. You'll cover most of the volume on day one, and add long-tail topics as real guests ask them.

Chat or voice at the desk

Chat

Just type your question.

Best for quiet spaces, privacy, and long answers like directions or menus.

Voice

Just ask out loud.

Best for busy lobbies and guests on the move. Replies are spoken and shown on screen.

Measuring the impact

Across partner properties, average front-desk queue time dropped from 6.2 to 2.9 minutes within the first month. The biggest gains came during the 2–4 PM check-in window, when repeat questions used to pile up.

Common questions

Will it replace my front-desk staff?

No. It takes the repetitive questions so your team has more time for guests who need a real person.

What if it doesn't know the answer?

It says so, and hands the guest to staff using the rules you set — no made-up answers.

Which languages does it support?

English, Japanese, Chinese, and Korean today, with more on the way.

Key takeaways
  • Most front-desk questions repeat — start with your top 20.
  • Group answers into topics so updates stay consistent.
  • Set clear hand-off rules for billing and complaints.
  • Use chat for quiet spaces, voice for busy lobbies.
